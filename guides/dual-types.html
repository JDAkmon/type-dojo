Dual-type damage: multiply both matchups

Calculate 4× weaknesses, ¼× resistances, neutral combinations, and immunities with worked examples.

A Pokémon with two defending types does not choose whichever type is more convenient. Look up the attacking move against each defending type, then multiply the two chart values. Use the same game era for both lookups. The order of the defending types does not affect the product.

Two weaknesses: 4×

Consider a Rock attack against Fire/Flying. Rock → Fire is 2×. Rock → Flying is also 2×. Multiplying gives 2 × 2 = 4×. The attack exploits both weaknesses. Do not add the entries: addition would fail on other combinations even though 2 + 2 coincidentally also equals four.

Two resistances: ¼×

Consider a Grass attack against Fire/Flying. Grass → Fire is ½× and Grass → Flying is ½×. The result is ½ × ½ = ¼×. Picture folding a sheet of paper in half and then in half again: one quarter remains.

A weakness and a resistance: 1×

Consider a Water attack against Grass/Ground. Water → Grass is ½×; Water → Ground is 2×. Multiply ½ × 2 = 1×. The two interactions balance to neutral. The target is neither Water-weak nor Water-resistant under this type-only calculation.

An immunity: 0×

Consider an Electric attack against Water/Ground. Electric → Water is 2×, but Electric → Ground is 0×. The product is zero. A fourfold weakness elsewhere cannot rescue an attack that meets a type immunity.

A neutral type leaves the other factor alone

For a Water attack against Rock/Flying, Water → Rock is 2× and Water → Flying is 1×. That gives 2×. A second defending type does not always create a dramatic result. Multiplication by one simply preserves the other entry.

A reliable three-step method

  1. Name the attack and write its value against the first defender type.
  2. Write its value against the second defender type independently.
  3. Multiply: zero first, then halves and doubles, with neutral factors left unchanged.

If you get stuck, practice each single-type relationship before repeating the combined question. Memorizing “Rock beats this species” can help recognition, but knowing both factors transfers to other Pokémon with the same typing.

For two distinct standard defending types, the possible chart multipliers are 0×, ¼×, ½×, 1×, 2×, and 4×. These are type multipliers, not predicted HP loss. Move power, stats, STAB, weather, abilities, and special mechanics remain separate.

Check yourself: Grass attacks Water/Ground. What is the multiplier?

Grass → Water is 2× and Grass → Ground is 2×, so the total is 4×. Compare it with Electric into the same defender, which gives zero.
