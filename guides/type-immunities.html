Type immunities: learn the zeroes without guessing

Understand the standard type immunities, their direction, and the generation changes that affect them.

An immunity is a zero in the type chart. It is stronger than resistance: multiplying by ½ reduces damage, while multiplying by zero removes the type-based damage entirely. Learn these pairs as directed statements, with the attack first and the defending type second.

The modern immunity checklist

  • Normal → Ghost: 0×. A normal strike passes through a ghost.
  • Fighting → Ghost: 0×. A punch cannot connect with the ghost.
  • Ghost → Normal: 0×. Think of a separate boundary in the opposite direction.
  • Electric → Ground: 0×. Picture the electric attack being grounded.
  • Ground → Flying: 0×. The target stays above the ground attack.
  • Poison → Steel: 0×. Picture poison failing to affect a metal target.
  • Psychic → Dark: 0×. A dark curtain blocks the psychic attack.
  • Dragon → Fairy: 0×. A fairy’s protection stops the dragon attack.

These are mnemonics for the standard chart, not rules that explain every move or special battle effect. They describe damaging type interactions, not a complete reference for status moves.

Do not reverse the arrow

Electric into Ground is 0×, but Ground into Electric is 2×. Ground into Flying is 0×, but Flying into Ground is 1×. Normal and Ghost happen to be immune to one another; that is a pair to learn, not a rule for all immunities.

Resistance can look deceptively similar in memory. Electric into Grass is ½×, not zero. Psychic into Steel is ½×, while Psychic into Dark is zero. Practice each immunity alongside a nearby resisted or neutral example so “not very effective” and “no effect” do not become the same answer in your head.

When a target has two types

A zero still wins. Electric against Water/Flying is 2 × 2 = 4×. Electric against Water/Ground is 2 × 0 = 0×. The Water weakness cannot cancel Ground’s immunity. Check for a zero before calculating the remaining factors.

Choose the correct era

Generation I has no Dark, Steel, or Fairy types. It also has the unusual Ghost → Psychic interaction: the original games use 0×. From Generation II, Ghost hits Psychic for 2×. Dark and Steel add their immunities in Generation II; Fairy adds its Dragon immunity in Generation VI.

Abilities, held items, and particular moves can create or override immunities in actual battles. This trainer intentionally isolates the standard type chart. When an in-game result differs, first check the game era and then the move’s special rules and the Pokémon’s ability.

Check yourself: does Water’s weakness make Electric hit Water/Ground for 2×?

No. Multiply both entries: 2 × 0 = 0×. The Ground immunity takes priority through multiplication.
