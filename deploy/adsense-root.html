A first lesson: read the arrow correctly

Type effectiveness compares an attacking move with the target’s defending type. Read “Water → Fire” as a Water attack hitting a Fire target. It gives a 2× multiplier. Reverse the arrow, and Fire → Water gives ½×. The Pokémon using the move can have a different type from that move, so identify the attack before looking up the defender.

Neutral damage is 1×; an immunity is 0×. Do not assume every advantage reverses into a resistance. Fighting → Normal is 2×, but Normal → Fighting is 1×. Learning the direction prevents a common beginner mistake.

Two defending types? Multiply.

A Rock attack against Fire/Flying meets two weaknesses: 2 × 2 = 4×. A Grass attack against the same types meets two resistances: ½ × ½ = ¼×. Water against Grass/Ground balances a resistance and a weakness: ½ × 2 = 1×.

An immunity changes the result completely. Electric attacks Water for 2×, but Ground for 0×, so Electric against Water/Ground is 2 × 0 = 0×. This calculation describes the standard type multiplier, not the number of HP an attack will remove.

Try it: Grass attacks Water/Ground. What happens?

Grass is super effective against both Water and Ground. Multiply 2 × 2 to get 4×. Compare this with Electric into the same target: its Ground immunity makes that attack 0×.
